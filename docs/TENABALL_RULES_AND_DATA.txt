TENABALL: RULES, ALGORITHMS AND DATA
====================================
As of update 97 (16 October 2026). Figures are counted from the live game, not estimated.
This is a summary: it says what the data is, where it came from and how much of it there is,
not the data itself.


1. WHAT THE GAME IS
-------------------
A Tenable-style football quiz. Each board asks for a top ten (or ten labelled answers). You name
answers one at a time; the board's "tower" scans up from the bottom and stops on your answer if it
is on the board.

Everything runs from one file, index.html, hosted on GitHub Pages. Accounts, online games, stats
and leagues use a small server (a Cloudflare Worker in server/).


2. WAYS TO PLAY (THE FOUR DOORS)
--------------------------------
- Solo play: just you and the board. Always Take turns. The shot clock can be switched off.
- H2H vs TenaBot: you against the computer. Any of the four game modes. TenaBot's level is
  Easy, Medium or Hard.
- Multiplayer local: 2 to 4 people passing one phone. Take turns or Penalty shootout.
- Multiplayer online: 2 to 4 people, each on their own phone, joined by a 5-character code or
  an invite link. Any of the four game modes. TenaBot never plays online.

Settings on the home screen:
- Rounds: 1, 3, 5 or 7 boards.
- Competitions: any mix of the 12 (section 9).
- Difficulty: Easy, Medium or Hard, or All levels (on by default).
- Shot clock: 30, 60 or 90 seconds a turn (Off in Solo play only).
- Time limit for the timed modes: 60, 90 or 120 seconds a round.
- Duplicate answers: Fill every match, or Fill one only.

Picking Penalty shootout hides rounds, competitions, difficulty and house rules, because there
are no boards.


3. TAKE TURNS (THE ORIGINAL MODE)
---------------------------------
- Each round is one board with 10 slots. Players take turns to lock in one answer.
- The scan: the tower lights each slot from 10th up to 1st, slowing as it climbs. It starts at
  95 ms a step and adds up to 20 ms more each step. It stops on the highest slot your answer
  fills, or runs off the top if your answer isn't there.
- Scoring: 1 point for every slot your answer fills. Filling the board's last empty slot is a
  "Tenable!", worth a 2 point bonus.
- Duplicate answers:
  - Fill every match: one guess fills every slot it matches, with a point each.
  - Fill one only: one guess fills one slot, and it must be named again for the next.
- Lives: 3 per player per round. Each of these costs a life and shows a yellow card: a wrong
  answer, a pass, or the shot clock running out. Losing the third life is a red card, and you're
  out until the next round.
- A round ends when the board is full or everyone is out. Points carry over between rounds; the
  highest total at full time wins.
- Who goes first: with 2 or more players, the order is shuffled once per game (a short animated
  shuffle). Each later round starts one place further down.
- Question changes: one "Different question" for every round in the game (5 in a 5-round game),
  shared across the game and usable at any round's intro.
- Ties at a board's cut-off: when players are level on the 10th-place figure, those slots share a
  "pool". Any of the tied names fills the shared places. The question says how many are tied
  (it never names them).


4. FASTEST ANSWER WINS
----------------------
- One shared board. Everyone answers at once against the round's time limit.
- The first right answer claims the slot in that player's colour.
- 1 point a slot, plus a bonus point for whoever claims the most (shared when tied).
- Wrong answers cost a life. A player who is out of lives stops answering.
- Your own answer still climbs the board with the scan before it's revealed; others' answers
  keep landing meanwhile.


5. BEAT THE CLOCK
-----------------
- Every player has their own copy of the board and the same time.
- Others' finds show on your board as name pills (with the stat), not the answer.
- At time up the board becomes a results grid: 1 point per answer you found, 2 for an answer
  nobody else found.
- Wrong answers cost a life. Letter boards are never used in this mode.


6. PENALTY SHOOTOUT (GAME MODE AND TIE-BREAK)
---------------------------------------------
Each kick is a two-option question: "Who has more...?" Right and you score; wrong and it's saved,
missed or off the woodwork.

The shootout follows the Laws of the Game:
- A coin toss (or a draw with 3 or 4 players) decides who shoots first.
- Five kicks each, taken in turn. It ends early as soon as a player can't be caught.
- If still level after five each, sudden death: one kick each per round until a round ends with
  someone ahead.
- With 3 or 4 players, anyone who can no longer catch the leader drops out.
- Everyone still in answers the same question each round. The easiest (most clear-cut) pairs are
  used first: a shuffled pool sorted by "gap".

The two uses:
- As a game mode: the goals scored are each player's score, and the winner tops the table.
- As a tie-break: when two or more players finish level on top at full time, a shootout settles
  it and the winner gets 1 extra point.

Online:
- Every phone plays the identical shootout. The toss, the questions and where each kick goes come
  from a seed every phone works out from the room code and game.
- Each player answers on their own phone. There are 20 seconds to answer, then the kick is
  missed. After 30 seconds the host can skip a quiet player.

TenaBot in a shootout:
- Hit rate = its level rate (0.40, 0.58 or 0.76) + 0.12 - 0.10 x (gap - 1), kept between 0.25
  and 0.95. So it's better on clear-cut pairs.

Animation (Craig's pixel art):
- Keeper: 87 frames, including 10 frames for saves straight down the middle.
- Coin: 9 frames. Ball spin: 10 frames.
- 14 kick outcomes, including six woodwork spots: each corner of the bar, and the top and the
  foot of each post.
- Football Manager style commentary.
- On a save, the keeper is timed to reach the ball when it has covered 90% of its flight.


7. HOW QUESTIONS ARE PICKED
---------------------------
Competition for each round:
- One of your ticked competitions, at random, never the same one twice in a row (unless only one
  is ticked).

Level:
- Your chosen level. With All levels on, a random level for each board (a third each).

Board within the competition and level:
1. Try your level first. If there's nothing left there, try the levels either side, then any
   level.
2. Leave out boards played recently on this phone and boards already used in this game. If none
   are left, the phone's "used" list for that group is reset.
3. Variety: every board belongs to one of 14 "families" (tables, scorers, trophies, managers,
   letters and so on). The phone remembers the last 12 families played. The pick favours a
   family not played for at least 3 boards, or the one played least recently, then picks a
   random board within it.
4. Club record scorers boards are drawn fresh each time from 51 clubs in three tiers. The clubs
   chosen travel in the board's id, so every phone rebuilds the same draw.

Online:
- Only the host picks. The chosen board's id travels to the other phones.


8. TENABOT
----------
Plays on the phone only, never online.

Base hit rate by level: Easy 0.40, Medium 0.58, Hard 0.76.

On each turn:
1. Chance of knowing an answer = base rate x (0.75 + 0.25 x share of slots still empty). It gets
   harder as the board fills.
2. If it knows one, it picks an empty slot weighted towards the top of the board (weight
   1 / (1 + 0.18 x position)), since the top is better known.
3. If it doesn't know one, it gives a near miss: a real name from another board in the same
   competition and of the same type. So it gets carded too. If there's no near miss, it passes.

Thinking time:
- Take turns: 1.5 to 3.5 seconds, plus 0.5 seconds for each level below Hard.
- Timed modes: a guess every 2.8 to 5.2 seconds by level, with a 30% chance of a near miss when
  it doesn't know one.

TenaBot keeps no stats. Games against it count in your win rate and in the vs TenaBot filter.

10. QUESTION RULES (CRAIG'S, ENFORCED)
--------------------------------------
- Every board has exactly 10 answers.
- No single answer may fill 4 or more slots on a board. The board is moved to other years or
  dropped.
- Periods:
  - Premier League player and goals data from 1992/93.
  - Champions League from 1992/93.
  - La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1 and the Scottish top flight from 2000/01.
  - Europa League (UEFA Cup included) from 2000/01.
  - World Cup and Euros player boards from the 1994 World Cup and Euro 96; nation boards cover
    every tournament.
  - Internationals: career boards are all time; "since 2000" boards from 1 January 2000.
- Every board states its period on a date line.
- Figures stop at the end of the last completed season (2025/26). Transfer fees run to the end of
  the latest window. Internationals stop at the 2026 World Cup final, 19 July 2026.
- Every board shows positions 1 to 10 down the left.
- Only data checked against a source is used. If it can't be verified, the board isn't built.
- A board's title, question and date line never name one of its answers. A test checks all
  1,237 boards.
- Every board's type must match its answers, checked by tests against the club, country and
  player lists.


11. THE DATA SETS BEHIND THE BOARDS
-----------------------------------
Premier League players (docs/data/pl_players/):
- Every player, 1992/93 to 2025/26: 34 seasons, 18,652 player-club-season rows, 5,113 different
  people (5,082 display names).
- Columns: apps (starts + sub appearances), goals (own goals excluded), position, nationality.
- Sources:
  - StatMuse FC: apps, goals and own goals.
  - 11v11.com: the independent second source for every row, and tie-breaker.
  - Wikipedia season pages: third source.
  - footballsquads.co.uk: positions and nationalities.
- Checks:
  - Every club-season reconciles: player goals + own goals by opponents = club goals (686 of
    686, difference 0).
  - 42 rows where sources still disagree are flagged with the figure used.
  - Every other row is confirmed by at least two sources.

Premier League results (docs/data/pl_results/):
- Every match, 1992/93 to 2025/26: 13,166 matches.
- Sources: football-data.co.uk, cross-checked against engsoccerdata. The two agree on every
  match in the 31 seasons they share.
- The build stops unless:
  - every season has 462 or 380 matches;
  - the table worked out from the results matches every club's points, allowing for the four
    points deductions.
- Used for the season and run records, and for the penalty questions on club points, wins and
  seasons.

Premier League season tables (PL and PL_PTS): 34 seasons of final tables with points after
deductions.

Premier League managers: 508 manager spells (docs/data/pl_managers.csv), used for the managers'
records boards.

Premier League records boards (scripts/build-pl-records.py):
- Covers managers, scoring records, transfer fees, relegations, promotions, penalties, awards and
  cups, hat-tricks, assists, keepers, cards, medals, crowds, shirt numbers, winners' squads and
  derby scorers.
- Some rows are held in the script; others are worked out from the data sets above.
- Sources and corrections: docs/PL_RECORDS_SOURCES.md.

Scottish top flight (docs/data/spfl/):
- 312 club-season table rows (26 seasons, 2000/01 to 2025/26, worked out from every result).
- 9,301 Transfermarkt player-season rows, cross-checked against 1,246 FitbaStats rows.
- 164 derby records, plus lists and transfers.
- Sources and decisions: SPFL_SOURCES.md.

La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1 and Top 5 (docs/data/europe/):
- One board file per pick, plus tables, players and notes, built by
  scripts/build-europe-records.py.

Champions League, Europa League, World Cup, Euros and Internationals (docs/data/cups/,
docs/data/intl/):
- 177 board files in all, each folder with NOTES files of sources and decisions.
- Club names must be in a list of 329 known clubs, and nations in a list of 71; the build stops
  on any it doesn't know.
- Former countries: West Germany counts as Germany, the Soviet Union and CIS as Russia, and
  Czechoslovakia as the Czech Republic, with the old name accepted too.
- Copa America boards were dropped, because every recent ten-edition window gives Brazil four
  places.

Champions League, World Cup and Euros final line-ups:
- The ten outfield starters of each Champions League finalist from 2017.
- The last four World Cup and Euros finals.

Ballon d'Or: most wins, winners by year, and the top ten of each vote from 2022.

Penalty questions (docs/penalties/):
- 614 raw pairs in 28 question types; 604 pairs kept.
- A pair is dropped if it couldn't be checked or the two come out level.
- Premier League figures (goals, appearances, goals per club for 10 clubs, club points, wins and
  seasons) are worked out from the player and results data sets above. Scottish seasons come
  from the season tables. Every other question uses values checked against two sources each
  (verified.json, VERIFY_NOTES.md).
- "gap" is how far apart the two sit in that question's ranking. By gap, the kept pairs are:
  209 neighbours (gap 1, the closest calls), 195 at gap 2 and 200 at gap 3.
- The 28 question types: Premier League goals, appearances, assists, clean sheets, club points,
  club wins, club seasons, and goals for each of Man Utd, Liverpool, Arsenal, Everton, West Ham,
  Aston Villa, Newcastle, Spurs, Man City and Chelsea; league titles across the top five leagues;
  Champions League goals; top five league goals; La Liga, Ligue 1 and Scottish top flight
  seasons; La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A and Ligue 1 club wins; and World Cup final appearances.


12. STATS
---------
Each finished game is folded into the player's stats by the same code on the phone and the
server.

Stats are kept:
- On the account, for a signed-in player.
- On the phone, for anyone else.
- TenaBot keeps none.

Top-level totals:
- games played, games with an opponent, wins, win streak, best streak, points, best score,
  full boards.

Detail filters (from the October 2026 update):
- All, Solo, vs TenaBot, Local, Online, Fastest answer wins, Beat the clock, Penalty shootout.
- Each filter has: games, wins, draws, streaks, points, boards played and completed, slots you
  found / others found / nobody found, right and wrong answers, passes, time-outs, cards, best
  round, best run, claims, unique finds, completion by competition and accuracy by level.
- In Penalty shootout games, goals count as right answers and misses as wrong.

Win rate and streaks:
- They count every game with an opponent (people or TenaBot), not games on your own.

Head to head:
- Your record against each person you've played (the 30 most recent kept).

Form:
- The last 10 results.


13. LEAGUES
-----------
Joining and limits:
- Signed-in players make a league (a name and a 6-character code) and invite with a link.
- 50 members a league, 10 leagues a player.
- There are no seasons; to get a fresh table, start a new league.

What counts:
- Every online game two or more members play together counts in every league they share, in any
  mode. One-phone, solo and TenaBot games don't count.

How the table works:
- Each round is a head to head against every other member in that game: a win, a draw on the same
  points, or a loss.
- Ranking: the share of rounds won, with a draw counting as half. Ties are split by games won,
  then rounds won.
- The Games filter ranks on games won.
- 5 head to heads are needed to be ranked, unless nobody has 5 yet.
- At full time each phone shows a line such as "Craig up to 1st, Phil down to 2nd".


14. BANTER AT FULL TIME
-----------------------
Every game ends with a sarcastic line, picked from 15 lists (140 lines in all). The rules work on
shares of the game's slots (rounds x 10).

Solo:
- Goes by your share: 0, poor, mid, good, great or perfect.

Multiplayer takes the first rule that fits, in this order:
1. everyone on 0
2. a tie for first
3. a table that found hardly anything
4. a chasm between first and last
5. a runaway winner
6. last place on 0
7. a close finish
8. otherwise, the winner's own score

The pick is seeded from the result, so every phone in an online game shows the same line, and a
phone skips lines it showed recently.


15. ONLINE PLAY
---------------
- The server's room keeps a numbered log of moves. Every phone replays the log through the same
  game rules, so the boards match.
- Only the host sends round, reveal, question change, next round, skip, time up and penalty skip.
- Players send guesses, passes, time-outs and penalty answers.
- Anything random (question picks, the order of play, the penalty seed) is decided once and sent,
  or worked out from the room code, so phones never drift apart.
- A player who reloads catches up by replaying the log quickly and quietly.


16. LOOK, SOUND AND ASSETS
--------------------------
Each competition has:
- its own colour theme;
- a faint watermark: 13 in all, one per competition plus the Ballon d'Or.

Badges and flags:
- 325 club crests embedded (327 names, since some answer to two names). All are re-sized so they
  look the same size.
- 69 circle flags; former countries get simple drawn flags.
- The full badge library (1,538 crests) is kept in assets/badges/.

Sound:
- The game's chimes, ticks and whistles are synthesised.
- Craig's recorded crowd plays underneath, in six kinds: cheer, big cheer, groan, boos, roar and
  applause. Each has its own level so the crowd sits under the chimes.
- Whistles: one blast for every card and for time up; three blasts only at full time.

Phones:
- The page never zooms and stays at full screen width.
- An iPhone Home Screen app reloads itself onto a newer version when there is one (version.txt).
