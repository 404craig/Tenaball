# Internationals notes, agent N1 (England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, Republic of Ireland)

Boards written to `boards/`: b01 to b04 for eng, sco, wal, nir and irl (20 boards), b05 (England managers), b06-sco, b06-wal, b06-irl and b06-nir (managers in order), b07 (England hat-tricks since 1992). 26 boards in all. None dropped.

## Periods

- All-time boards: every full international to 19 July 2026 (the 2026 World Cup final). September and October 2026 games are left out. England's last game before the cut-off was the World Cup third-place match against France on 18 July 2026; Scotland's was Brazil v Scotland on 24 June 2026 (out in the group); Wales played last on 6 June 2026, Northern Ireland on 7/8 June 2026 (France away) and Ireland on 5 June 2026 (Canada). Wales, Northern Ireland and Ireland were not at the World Cup.
- Since-2000 boards: matches from 1 January 2000 to 19 July 2026 only, counted match by match.

## Sources and method

1. **Transfermarkt per-game data.** `https://www.transfermarkt.com/ceapi/performance-game/<player id>` returns every match a player has played (club and country) with date, team id and goals. For each nation I took every player in the top 100 of Transfermarkt's all-time caps list and top 100 of its all-time scorers list (`/rekordspieler/verein/<id>/sort/einsaetze.desc` and `tore.desc`), born 1960 or later, and counted matches where the player's team was the national team (England 3299, Scotland 3380, Wales 3864, Northern Ireland 5674, Ireland 3509) and his participation was "played". Counts: to 19 July 2026, from 1 January 2000 to 19 July 2026, and after the cut-off (to show what was excluded). These are in `N1_<code>_players.csv`. Local match dates are used for the cut-off.
2. **Wikipedia, as it stood at the cut-off.** I read each records page by the revision live on 19 or 20 July 2026 (via the page history), so figures are at the cut-off, not today: England records and statistics (revision of 19 July 2026), Scotland records and statistics (1 July 2026, updated after 24 June), Wales records and statistics (11 June 2026, updated to 6 June), Northern Ireland records and statistics (12 June 2026), Republic of Ireland national football team (16 July 2026, updated after 5 June), List of Wales international footballers (6 June 2026), List of Republic of Ireland international footballers (18 July 2026). The current pages were also read; they include September and October 2026 games, which matches the Transfermarkt "after the cut-off" counts.
3. **RSSSF record international players pages** (eng, scot, wal, nil, ier). Scotland (last match 24 June 2026), Northern Ireland (7 June 2026) and Ireland (5 June 2026) are exactly at the cut-off. England's is to November 2025 and Wales's to November 2025, so they were used for retired players only.
4. **England Football Online** (`englandfootballonline.com`, http only), updated to match 1090 (France, 18 July 2026): most appearances, goalscorers, hat-tricks and managers.
5. **Player articles on Wikipedia** for the caps-and-goals-by-year tables of players whose careers straddle 2000: Beckham, Lampard, Owen, Bellamy, Hartson, Aaron Hughes, Robbie Keane, Given, Kilbane, Duff, Henderson; and infobox totals for Washington, Feeney, Ballard, Whyte, McGinn and Jonny Evans.
6. **Managers:** Wikipedia's manager pages or sections and Transfermarkt's staff history (`/mitarbeiterhistorie/verein/<id>`); England also EFO's chronology.

eu-football.info returned empty pages from this machine, so it wasn't used.

## Decisions and disagreements

- **Association counts are used where sources differ by one,** following Wikipedia and RSSSF (FA, SFA, FAW, IFA, FAI counts), with the other figure in the notes. None of these changes who is in a ten, except where a pool is used:
  - England: EFO credits Owen 41, Lampard 28 and Rooney 52 (it reassigns a goal against Wales in 2004 and one other); FA, Wikipedia, RSSSF and Transfermarkt have 40, 29 and 53. Henderson 91 caps (EFO, Wikipedia) against 90 (Transfermarkt). Sterling 20 goals (Wikipedia, Transfermarkt, EFO) against 19 (RSSSF).
  - Scotland: Hughie Gallacher 24 (Wikipedia) or 23 (RSSSF, Transfermarkt); 3rd either way. Robert Hamilton 15/14/15, James McFadden 15/13/14 and Mo Johnston 14/14/14 (Wikipedia/RSSSF/Transfermarkt), so places 9 and 10 of the all-time scorers are a pool of those three. Ryan Christie 10 goals (Wikipedia, Transfermarkt) or 9 (RSSSF). Darren Fletcher 80 caps (Wikipedia, RSSSF) or 81 (Transfermarkt); Kenny Miller 69 or 68.
  - Wales: Bale 41 (FAW, Wikipedia, RSSSF) against 40 (Transfermarkt). Ashley Williams 86 caps against 87.
  - Northern Ireland: Billy Gillespie 13 (Wikipedia) or 12 (RSSSF); both give the same ten names. Jonny Evans 107 caps (Wikipedia, RSSSF) against 108 (Transfermarkt).
  - Ireland: Staunton 102, McGeady 93 and Cascarino 88 caps (Wikipedia, RSSSF) against 103, 92 and 89 (Transfermarkt). Ian Harte 11 goals (RSSSF, Transfermarkt; 2 in 1996, so 9 from 2000) against 12 (Wikipedia); he is in the since-2000 ten whether he has 9 or 10.
- **Bounds instead of exact counts** where only one source counts a straddler's caps from 2000: Maik Taylor (first cap March 1999; Northern Ireland played 8 games in 1999) has between 80 and 88 from 2000, so he is in the ten either way; 80 (Transfermarkt) is shown.
- **Pools:** b01-eng 10th (Woodward, Lampard, 29); b01-sco 9th and 10th (Hamilton, McFadden, Johnston); b03-sco 10th (Maloney, Snodgrass, Shankland, Boyd, 7); b01-wal 9th and 10th (Earnshaw, Cliff Jones, Mark Hughes, 16); b02-wal 10th (Ledley, Allen, 77); b07 places 4 to 10 (the eight players with one hat-trick). Northern Ireland's and Wales's since-2000 scorers came out clean (three on 6 fill 8 to 10 for Northern Ireland; James and Koumas on 10 fill 9 and 10 for Wales).
- **Managers:** labelled by the year each took up the post. Caretakers out. England: Eriksson 2001 and Capello 2008 (took charge January 2001 and January 2008; Transfermarkt dates the appointments to 2000 and December 2007); Tuchel 2025 (appointed October 2024, started 1 January 2025). Wales: Toshack's second spell 2004 (appointed 12 November 2004; Wikipedia's table shows his first match in 2005); Rob Page 2022, when he was made permanent (interim from November 2020). Northern Ireland: Michael O'Neill's first spell 2011 (appointed December 2011, first match February 2012). Start points: Scotland from Andy Roxburgh (1986), Wales from Toshack's first spell (1994), Ireland from Eoin Hand (1980), Northern Ireland from Danny Blanchflower (1976); each gives exactly ten up to July 2026. Steve Clarke resigned after the 2026 World Cup and Sebastien Pocognoli started in August 2026, after the period. Alan Kelly Sr. (Ireland, one match in 1980, before Hand) is not on Transfermarkt's list and falls before the start point.
- **England hat-tricks since 1992:** 17 hat-tricks by 11 players from August 1992 to 18 July 2026 (EFO and Wikipedia agree), so the board is ranked, not open: Kane 5, Owen 2, Saka 2, then eight with one sharing seven places.
- **Germany note** doesn't apply to these nations. Northern Ireland's all-time scorers include the pre-1921 Ireland team (Olphie Stanfield, Billy Gillespie), as the IFA, Wikipedia and RSSSF count them; the brief says so.
