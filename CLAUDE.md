# Tenaball: notes for Claude Code

Tenable-style football quiz. Everything lives in `index.html` (HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file, assets embedded as data URIs). `tenable-animation.html` is a standalone preview of the end-of-game trophy sequence and should stay in step with the game's version.

## Question rules (set by Craig, keep these)

- Every question has exactly 10 answers.
- No single answer may fill 4 or more slots on a board. Move the question to years that avoid it, otherwise drop it.
- Player and goals data counts only from the start of the Premier League, 1992/93. Champions League data from 1992/93, when it became the Champions League.
- World Cup and Euros team questions (winners, hosts and so on) can cover every tournament since each competition began.
- Every question states the period it covers, shown as a date line under the question.
- Figures stop at the end of the last completed season (2025/26 for now), never part-way through a season. Transfer fees may run to the end of the latest window.
- Every board shows positions 1 to 10 down the left, including year-based boards.
- Only use data that has been checked against a source. If data can't be verified, stop rather than guess.

## Writing style

- Never use em dashes in any text in the game or in docs.

## Structure pointers

- Question data: `PL`, `T5`, `UCL_W`, `UCL_R`, `EXTRA_Q`, `EXTRA_Q2`, `EXTRA_Q3`, `EXTRA_Q4` and the helper builders near them. `EXTRA_Q4` is the October 2026 Premier League records boards (managers, scoring records, transfer fees in and out, each club's biggest signings, and relegations, promotions, penalties, manager games, club records from the season tables (`pl-rec-*`: top-four finishes, yo-yo clubs, fewest points and so on, read from `PL` and `PL_PTS`), season series (best promoted club, the champions' top scorer), and boards worked out from the player dataset: appearances per club, scorers and appearances by nationality and by decade, keepers, seasons at one club, defenders' and midfielders' goals, scorers since 2000; plus Craig's October 2026 picks: season and run records from every result in `docs/data/pl_results/` (built by `scripts/build-pl-results.py`), awards and cups, hat-tricks, assists, keepers and cards, managers' records worked out from `docs/data/pl_managers.csv`, medals, transfers, crowds, shirt numbers, winners' squads and derby scorers), built into index.html by `scripts/build-pl-records.py`, which holds every row or reads `docs/data/pl_players/pl_players.csv`; edit the rows there and run it from the repo root; sources and corrections are in `docs/PL_RECORDS_SOURCES.md`. A tie at a board's cut-off uses a pool (`pool` and `alts` on the slots), so any of the tied names fills the shared places. A final pass re-cuts or removes boards that break the 4-slot rule. Every board's `type` must match its answers (`club`, `person`, `nation`, `place`): it sets the placeholder, the suggestions, the answer dictionary and TenaBot's near misses, and answers on `person` boards are added to the list of people, so a mis-typed board looks valid to itself. `labelled` and `series` default to `person`; pass `type="club"` for club answers. tests/game.test.mjs checks every board against the club, country and player lists.
- Scottish top flight boards from Craig's October 2026 picks (`EXTRA_Q5`, plus open boards `SPFL_OPEN` that take any name on their list): tables records, derby scorers, managers, captains, PFA Scotland awards, cup runners-up, relegation and promotion, European final line-ups, transfer fees, and scorers, appearances and assists since 2000 for the big clubs, built by `scripts/build-spfl-records.py` from `docs/data/spfl/` (season tables worked out from every result, Transfermarkt player seasons cross-checked against FitbaStats, derbies, lists and transfers; sources and decisions in `docs/data/spfl/SPFL_SOURCES.md`). Edit the data or the rows there and run it from the repo root. Open boards accept a name if `q.open.fits(name)` says so (the Premier League ones also need the name in `PLP`).
- La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1 and Top 5 boards from Craig's October 2026 picks (`EXTRA_Q6`, open boards `EURO_OPEN`, and `EURO_PEOPLE`, every 2025/26 squad player plus everyone with 30+ league goals or 200+ league games since 2000/01, so wrong answers are recognised): 2025/26 tables, scorers, assists, squads and club goals; scorers, appearances, hat-tricks, titles and derby scorers since 2000/01; cups, awards and title-winning managers by window; club managers in order; transfer fees; British and Irish players; grounds. Built by `scripts/build-europe-records.py` from `docs/data/europe/` (each agent's board JSON, tables, players and notes; see its README). These leagues count from 2000/01, not 1992/93 (Craig, 5 October 2026). The script sets each board's id from its pick and family, moves the best-known boards to easy (`EASY`), and tidies period lines.
- Champions League, Europa League, World Cup and Euros boards from Craig's October 2026 picks are built by the same script (`scripts/build-europe-records.py`) into `EXTRA_Q6` and `EURO_OPEN`, from `docs/data/cups/ucl/`, `docs/data/cups/uel/` and `docs/data/intl/{wc,euro}/` (each folder's `boards/*.json` and `NOTES_*.md` hold the data, sources and decisions). Periods: Champions League from 1992/93, Europa League (UEFA Cup included) from 2000/01, World Cup and Euros player boards from the 1994 World Cup and Euro 96, nation boards every tournament (West Germany counts as Germany, the Soviet Union and CIS as Russia, Czechoslovakia as the Czech Republic, each with the old name as an alt). Club names on cup boards must be in `docs/data/cups/clubs_all.txt` and nations in `docs/data/intl/nations.txt`; the script stops on any it doesn't know. The Europa League (`uel`) is in `ALL_CATS` from 6 October 2026; a phone that had every competition ticked before then gets it ticked once.
- Internationals (`intl`, Craig's October 2026 picks, in `ALL_CATS` and the International group beside World Cup and Euros): 81 boards from `docs/data/intl/intl/boards/` built by the same script (`scripts/build-europe-records.py`), with each research agent's notes in `NOTES_N1.md` to `NOTES_N5.md`. Each nation's all-time and since-2000 top scorers and caps, home nations' managers, England hat-tricks, boards across nations, the Africa Cup of Nations and the FIFA ranking. Career boards count every full international (all time, so they break the 1992/93 rule by Craig's choice); since-2000 boards count from 1 January 2000; everything stops at the 2026 World Cup final, 19 July 2026 (move it on after the next summer). `people_*.txt` there are each nation's top 30s, recognised so near misses get a card. `CANON` in the script maps a board's short name to the game's spelling (Ronaldo to Ronaldo Nazario). Theme: green (`body[data-cat="intl"]`); watermark: the flags ball from Craig's photo (`assets/watermarks/intl_photo.jpg`, cut to `intl.png`). Phones that saved their setup before `v: 4` with every older competition ticked get the Europa League and Internationals ticked once. Copa America boards were dropped: every recent ten-edition window gives Brazil four places.
- Every Premier League player, 1992/93 to 2025/26 (5,082 names, Craig's dataset in `docs/data/pl_players/`, see its README for sources and checks) is embedded as `PL_PLAYERS` by `scripts/build-pl-players.py` (re-run it after updating the data). Each becomes a recognised name (`PLP`, `PEOPLE`); the game's own spelling wins where it differs (`CANON` in the script: Son, Zagorakis, Kanu, Juninho, Salva). Letter boards (`pl-letter-a` and so on, `LETTER_BOARDS`) are open boards (`q.open`): any player whose surname begins with the letter counts (both the surname's first letter and the last word's, so De Bruyne counts for B and D), answers fill from 10th up, there's no stat, and at the end empty places show well-known examples (most appearances, `openExamples`). Rare letters share a board (I, Q or U; X, Y or Z). Boards with 200 or more players are easy, the rest medium. Each open board has `q.open.fits(name)`, which decides if a name counts. The same script also builds the club open boards (`PL_OPEN`, `PL_CLUBS`): surname letters at one club (`pl-letter-lfc-s`), players who played for two clubs (`pl-both-eve-mu`), and boards with their own list of answers (`names`, with a `miss` note: the 100-goal and 500-game clubs, and each big club's forwards, midfielders and defenders, `pl-open-lfc-fwd`); each player's clubs are stored per player, so two players who share a name don't combine their clubs.
- Drawn boards: club record scorers (`CLUB_REC`, every Premier League club, in tiers 0 to 2; the records build script checks each row against the player dataset) aren't a fixed ten. `pickQuestion` swaps each level's board (`pl-club-rec-0` to `-2`) for a fresh draw (`drawClubRec`), and the clubs chosen travel in the board's id, for example `pl-club-rec-1:0.4.12...`. Look boards up with `findQ(id)`, not `Q.find`, so every phone in an online game rebuilds the same draw.
- Difficulty: `q.level` (0 easy, 1 medium, 2 hard); the home screen slider picks the level. Older boards get theirs from the rule block after `/* difficulty` in `index.html`; the records boards from `scripts/build-pl-records.py` (`RELEVEL` moves boards between levels); club open boards are medium for one club and hard for two. Rebalanced on 3 October 2026 to 52 easy, 128 medium and 107 hard Premier League boards: under All levels each level gets a third of the picks, so a thin easy level repeats quickly. The All levels switch (`cfg.allLevels`, on by default; phones that saved their setup before it became the default, with no `v: 2` in `tenaball-setup`, get it switched on once) greys the slider out and `pickQuestion` picks a level at random for each board, so each level gets a fair share. Online rooms send All levels as level 3.
- Competitions: the home screen's box opens a slide-up panel of toggles (`#compSheet`, grouped by `COMP_GROUPS`, with quick picks). The ticked list is `cfg.cats`; `nextCat` picks one per round, never the same twice running. Ticks and All levels are saved on the phone (`tenaball-setup`), and the host's travel to every phone in an online game as `settings.cats`. Flags and badges are drawn SVG (`compIcon`), not emoji, which don't show on Windows or show England and Scotland on many Android phones. A competition in `COMP_GROUPS` but not in `ALL_CATS` (none for now) shows greyed out as "Coming later".
- Home screen: four doors (`.door`, `data-door`): Solo play (`solo`, just you and the board), H2H vs TenaBot (`bot`, the only place TenaBot plays), Multiplayer local (`h2h`, 2 to 4 people passing one phone) and Multiplayer online (`online`, the old online menu, shown only with a server), with a fixed tab bar (`#tabBar`: Stats, Share, Account; no Play tab). `setView` switches the views inside `#setup` (`doors`, `door` for `#doorSet`, `share`, `acct`, `stats`); `openDoor(d)` sets `cfg.door` and `homeSync()` shows that door's fields. H2H vs TenaBot has TenaBot's level (`cfg.botLevel`) and, like online, a game mode (`cfg.mode`: `turns` Take turns, `first` Fastest answer wins (called First touch in the code and earlier docs), `clock` Beat the clock; `modeNow()`); the timed modes swap the shot clock for a time limit per round (`cfg.time`, 60, 90 or 120 seconds). Multiplayer local has 2 to 4 seats (`cfg.seats`), people only. The shot clock is 30, 60 or 90 seconds; Off (`#clockOff`) shows only in Solo play, and `homeSync` turns an Off left over from Solo play into 30. Mode, time and bot level are saved in `tenaball-setup`. Stats record the TenaBot door as `solo` with a bot opponent. The Account tab holds name, email, PIN change (server `/pin`) and sign out.
- Game modes: `G.mode`. Take turns is the original loop. First touch and Beat the clock are live rounds (`G.phase = "live"`, `beginLive`, the round's clock in the shot clock ring, `liveLockIn`, `applyLiveGuess`, `applyTimeUp`, `endLiveRound`): First touch has one shared board where a slot takes its claimer's gradient (`--g1` to `--g4`, `.pc1` to `.pc4`), 1 point a slot and a bonus point for the most slots; Beat the clock gives each player their own board (`p.B`), others' finds show as name pills (`.upill`) with the stat, and at time up the board becomes a results grid (`drawResults`; 1 point an answer, 2 for one nobody else found). Wrong answers cost a life in both; cards flash without blocking typing. Your own answer climbs the board with the same slowing scan as Take turns before it's revealed: the move is applied at once (so phones agree), and only this phone's view holds it back (`G.held`, `G.revealing`; `drawTower` and `renderPlayers` hide the held slots, points and lost life until the scan stops). You can't lock in another answer until it stops; others' answers keep landing meanwhile, and the round's end waits for it. Safety net (October 2026, after a frozen answer box on Craig's iPhone that never reproduced in tests): the reveal chain can't break (`safe`, a 6 second cap on a scan, `.catch`), `applyGuess`, `applyPass` and `applyTimeout` are wrapped so an error unsticks the round (`unstick`), `botTurn` comes back if the game was busy and `watchdog` (once a second) releases a hold older than 8 seconds, re-enables a dead input and re-prompts a TenaBot turn whose timer never came back. Lock in during a hold says to hang on; Pass is hidden on TenaBot's turn; an `input` event that leaves the picked name in the box keeps `G.pick`. Letter boards are never drawn for Beat the clock. Both end each round with a slide-up points sheet (`#roundSheet`, `showRoundSheet`); ✕ slides it away and the page shows a points bar (tap to bring it back) with Next round or Final scores in the usual place. Online, guesses go to the room at any time and the host's phone sends `timeup` when its clock runs out; keep `applyLiveGuess` and `applyTimeUp` deterministic like the other apply functions. A player's colour index (`p.ci`) stays with them whatever the order.
- Who goes first (`whoGoesFirst`): taking turns with 2 or more players shuffles the order once per game (pills under a blur, then slowing; a tap skips; no reshuffle). Online the host draws the order and sends it in the first `round` move (`data.order`).
- TenaBot (`p.bot = { level }`, `botTurn`, `botLive`, `botAnswer`, `botMiss`): plays on the phone only, never online. Hit rate by level (`BOT_HIT`), the top of a board better known than the bottom, near misses from other boards in the same competition, so it gets carded too. `window.TENABALL_BOT_PACE` speeds it up for tests. TenaBot keeps no stats.
- Stats: `server/src/stats.js` (`bumpStats`, `mergeStats`, `cleanRec`) folds one game's record (`gameRec`: door, mode, opponents, every board with slots you found, others found and nobody found) into a player's stats. The same code runs on the server and in `index.html` between the `stats.js` markers: after editing it run `node scripts/embed-stats.mjs` (tests/stats.test.mjs checks they match). The old totals stay at the top level; the detail (filters All, Solo, vs TenaBot, H2H phone, H2H online, First touch, Beat the clock) lives under `x` and starts from the first game after the update. Win rate counts every game with an opponent, people or TenaBot (`people` in the stats means games with an opponent); games on your own don't count. The Stats tab's All filter and Share my stats split the record into on your own, against friends and against TenaBot (`splitOf`). Stats saved before 5 October 2026 left TenaBot games out; `upgradeStats` adds them in once (`x.v = 2`). The Stats tab (`drawStatsPage`) shows tiles, boards, boards completed by competition, answers, bests, accuracy, head to head, badges, Share my stats (`shareStats`), Compare (friends starred from the last 10 signed-in online opponents, server `/friends`, `/friends/star`, `/friends/stats`, or players on this phone) and Reset my stats (`/stats/reset` for an account).
- Home screen layout: each door's settings sit in three panels (`.homegrp`): Who's playing (players, names, opponent), The game (game mode, rounds, competitions, difficulty) and House rules (shot clock or time limit, duplicate answers: Fill every match or Fill one only, with a line under the pills saying what the chosen one does). The home screen is sized close to the game screens: the logo is 250px wide on a 390px phone (64vw, against 46vw in a game) and labels, pills and boxes are a touch smaller than the defaults (the block under "home screen: a little smaller" in the CSS). Its watermark is the ball from the Tenaball logo (`#setup .wmk`, `assets/watermarks/home.png`), at the top right below the logo and fainter than the competition marks (5 percent).
- Question changes: `G.refreshLeft` starts at `cfg.rounds` (one per round, Craig, 6 October 2026), shared across the game and usable at any round's intro (`#refreshBtn`, `applyRefresh`).
- Questions to the player: never the phone's own `confirm()`, `prompt()` or `alert()` (grey iOS boxes; Craig, 9 October 2026). Use `ask(text, { input, value, placeholder, max, upper, ok, cancel, danger })` (`#askBox`), which resolves true or the typed text for OK and null for Cancel. Tests answer it through `window.TENABALL_ASK` (set by `tests/lib.mjs`; `TENABALL_ASK_VALUE` for text) and read what was asked from `window.__asked`.
- Home screen help: each section heading has a small i (`.finfo`, `data-info`) that opens `#infoSheet`, a slide-up panel like the competitions one, with text from `INFO`. How to play at the bottom opens the same panel with the rules (kept in the `#howText` template). Latest updates, beside it, opens the changelog (the `#updatesText` template): one line per merged pull request, numbered by the PR and grouped by date, newest first. Add a line for every PR that changes the game (skip ones that only touch docs or idea lists). With each new line, set `version.txt` (repo root) to its number: the game compares it with the top of Latest updates (`BUILD`, `checkVersion`) when it opens or comes back to the front, and reloads itself on the home screen when there's a newer one, because an iPhone Home Screen app otherwise keeps an old copy in memory; `tests/stats.test.mjs` checks the two match. Rounds can be 1, 3, 5 or 7.
- Club badges: `badge(name)` draws a crest from the badge pack when the club has one, otherwise the old two-colour circle. Crests are transparent 72px PNGs embedded as base64 in their own `<script>` (`BADGE_IMG`), found by the game's own club name through `BADGE_FILE` (`crestKey`, which ignores case and accents and follows aliases). A pack row with two names (`RB Salzburg|Red Bull Salzburg`) answers to both. Arles, Lleida, Merida and Salamanca have no badge and keep the circle. Crests are 32px on the board and 26px in suggestions, each in a fixed cell (`.bcell`) so crests, circles and flags line up, and centred with the text in suggestion pills. Every embedded crest is re-made from the 128px masters by `scripts/size-badges.py` so they all look the same size: each is scaled so the square root of its width times height is the same (round crests about 25px across on the board), capped so its long side fits, and centred; `TRIM` in the script shrinks a couple of heavy wide crests. Run it again after adding a badge. No backing or white circle behind any crest (Craig dropped it), so very dark crests such as Spurs, Juventus and Derby are hard to see on dark rows; that is accepted. A missed row fades its name to 65 percent but not its crest (the name is wrapped in `.nm`). Crests and UEFA logos are trademarks: fine for this private family game, not for a public release. To add or rename a badge, update `assets/badges/clubs.csv` (its `game_name` must match the game's name) and regenerate `BADGE_FILE` and `BADGE_IMG`, then run `scripts/size-badges.py`.
- Flags and competition icons: country boards use circle flags from the pack (`NATION_FLAG`, `FLAG_IMG`, `badgeN`), 24px on the board and 19px in suggestions; Czechoslovakia, the Soviet Union and Yugoslavia have no flag in the pack, so `FORMER_FLAG` draws simple circle flags for them. The home screen's competition box and panel (`compIcon`) use the same circle flags, a circle of five stripes for Top 5 Leagues, the pack's Champions League starball and Europa League trophy (`LOGO_IMG`), and a drawn World Cup trophy on a round base. Year-labelled boards (`.tower.shortlab`) get a narrow label column so names fit, and names on those boards stay on one line with an ellipsis.
- Badge library: every badge we have (1,538 crests at 72px and 128px masters, every flag, the competition logos, and the CSV and JSON name tables) is stored in `assets/badges/` (see its README). The game embeds only what it uses. Brief for competition background artwork: `docs/BACKGROUND_ARTWORK_BRIEF.md`.
- Leagues (Phil's idea, built 8 October 2026; design agreed with Craig in the leagues artifact): signed-in players make a league (a name, nothing else; a 6-character code, an admin) and invite with a link (`?league=CODE`, joined after sign-in through `PENDING.league`). Every online game two or more members play together counts in every league they share, in any mode; one-phone, solo and TenaBot games don't. Each signed-in phone reports the game at full time (`leagueReport`: each account player's points per round from `p.g.rounds`, boards completed per level, and the winner after penalties) to `/leagues/result` with the key `code:game:created` (the room's `created` is in `publicRoom`); the server keeps the first report per league and returns a line for each league ("Craig up to 1st · Phil down to 2nd", `leagueMoves`), shown under the banter (`#leagueLines`). The table (`leagueTable` in `server/src/leagues.js`, embedded in `index.html` between the `leagues.js` markers by `node scripts/embed-stats.mjs`, checked by `tests/stats.test.mjs`) counts each round as a head to head against every other member in the game (win, draw on the same points, loss); filters All (P, W, Rounds won-drawn-lost, %), Games (ranked on games won) and Rounds, and All time, 7 days or 24 hours. 5 head to heads to be ranked, unless nobody has 5 yet. The leader's row is a gold pill with the line crown; arrows show places moved since this phone last looked (`tenaball-lg-seen`). Tapping a row opens form (last five rounds) and boards completed by level: gold for this league, lilac (`--allc`) for all play, out of every board at that level; all play comes from the stats' per-level completed count (`lvl[i][2]`, counted from 8 October 2026). Compare and Full stats use `/friends/stats`, which now also lets league mates see each other. The Leagues tab (`#lgTab`, `#leaguesPane`, `drawLeagues`, `drawLeague`) shows only with a server; Multiplayer online has a Your leagues row (`#lgOnline`). The admin can remove a member or hand over admin; when the admin leaves, the longest-standing member takes over, and an empty league is deleted. Limits: 50 members a league, 10 leagues a player. No seasons (Craig): start a new league for a fresh table.
- Penalty shootout (`PENS`, its own `<script>` near the end of `index.html`, from Craig's shootout build saved in `docs/penalties/`): when two or more players finish level on top, `endGame` runs `PENS.run(level, opts)` before the table, and the winner gets a point (`G.pens` holds the winner and score for the label and the share message; each player's kicks scored are in `p.pens`). Each kick is a two-option "who has more" question (`PEN_DATA`, 604 pairs in 28 question types, built by `scripts/build-penalties.py` from `docs/penalties/questions_raw.json`: Premier League figures are worked out from the player and results datasets, the rest from `docs/penalties/verified.json`, checked against two sources each, see `VERIFY_NOTES.md`; a pair that can't be checked or comes out level is dropped). `gap` is how far apart the two sit in that question's ranking (1 is neighbours). Five kicks each, then sudden death; with 3 or 4 players anyone who can't catch the leader drops out. The keeper is Craig's pixel art (`PEN_GK`, 77 frames), with a coin toss (`PEN_COIN`), a sprung net and Football Manager style commentary (two centred lines, no round label, Craig 8 October 2026). The ball spins through Craig's ten frames (`PEN_SPIN`, `assets/penalties/ball/spin_*.png`, cut to 22px; `SPIN`, `spinTo`, `spinStop`): it whips up on the kick, keeps turning in the net and winds down to a stop as it drops. Saves straight down the middle use Craig's ten centre frames (`assets/penalties/keeper_centre/`, appended to `PEN_GK` as 77 to 86 by the build script at his keeper's pixel scale): a low shot (`C low`, `catchLowC`) lands at his feet and he scoops it up; a chest-high one (`C mid`, `catchChest`) goes into his arms; he stands holding it. Off the woodwork (Craig's sketches): six spots (`WOOD`: each corner of the bar, the top and the foot of each post); `woodHit` flashes a drawn pixel bang (`bangAt`: red, orange and yellow star, white core, speed lines trailing back along the shot, sparks) for about a third of a second, then the ball carries on away from the goal, 5% bigger each step, fading gently. `window.TENABALL_PEN_FORCE` (a spot such as "C low", or a woodwork spot such as "barR") makes every kick go there, for checking the keeper. On a save the ball flies a little slower and the keeper is timed to reach it when it has covered 90% of its path (the flight eases out, so timing to its end left the ball looking in before the keeper arrived). The backdrop is option C from the backdrop artifact (Craig, 8 October 2026): inside the goal's own SVG (still 200 by 150 units, so dives and the ball's flight are unchanged), the stand and crowd (`kCrowd` pattern) run far up and to the sides and fade into the page through an SVG mask (`kFade`), and the grass (`kGrass`) runs to the bottom of the screen with the 18-yard box and arc, under a dark fade (`kScrim`) so the question stays readable; the goal sits at z-index 1 and the rest of the card at 2, so the grass stays behind the text; the gear opens gloves mode, frame blending and commentary on or off. Online, every phone plays the same shootout: the toss, the questions and where each kick goes come from a seed every phone works out from the room code, game and scores, and each player answers on their own phone as a `pen` move (20 seconds, then it's a miss; after 30 seconds the host can skip a quiet player with `penskip`). TenaBot answers with its usual hit rate, better on clear-cut pairs. Tests set `TENABALL_PEN_PACE` and `TENABALL_PEN_AUTO` (in `tests/lib.mjs`) so a draw's shootout plays itself in a moment. Craig's admin account (`ADMIN_EMAILS`, `isAdmin`) gets a practice link on the Account tab (`PENS.test()`, `#penTest`: 2 to 4 players, any of them TenaBot, auto-play); it's only a shortcut and nothing it does is privileged or counted in stats.

Found rows look the same in every theme (no coloured bar across the top on the Champions League board).
- Premier League season tables (`pl-top`, `pl-mid`, `pl-bot`) show each club's final points (`PL_PTS`, same order as `PL`, after deductions). Sources and checks are in `docs/PL_POINTS_SOURCES.md`. Add the new season's points when adding a season to `PL`, or the page stops with an error.
- Phone status bar: iOS Safari paints it one solid colour taken from a fixed full-width strip at the top (`.topfade`, and each overlay's own `::before`); Android Chrome uses `<meta name="theme-color">`, which `syncChrome()` keeps on the colour at the top of the screen. Safari ignores a strip 10px or thinner, one below the page (negative z-index), or a see-through one that ignores taps, so the strips are 16px, z-index 0, and the page (`.toplogo`, `.wrap`) sits above them at z-index 1. Each theme sets `--topc` (its average top-edge colour) and each full-screen overlay sets `--band`. Safari 26 also tints its bottom toolbar from what's fixed at the bottom of the screen, so `.botfade` is a 12px solid strip in `--bg2` there. A new theme or overlay needs one of these, or a band shows under the status bar. The keyboard and Safari's bottom toolbar shrink the visible viewport but not the layout one, so `fitBottom` (visualViewport events) sets `--kb` to the gap and the dock, the points sheet, the overlays (cards, banner, intro), the bottom strip and `#game`'s bottom padding sit above it; the scan's camera (`boardView`, `viewTargetFor`) aims at the middle of the visible area above the dock; in the live modes the box is disabled and blurred while your answer climbs (as `applyGuess` does in Take turns), which is what makes the iPhone keyboard drop so the board is in view, and it's re-enabled at the reveal (iOS won't reopen the keyboard without a tap); a live round's end blurs the input so the keyboard drops; the viewport meta also asks Android Chrome to resize the page for the keyboard (`interactive-widget=resizes-content`).
- Sound: the game's own sounds are synthesised (`sfx`, `tone`, `bell`, `whistle`). Craig's recorded crowd takes (`assets/sounds/crowd/`, embedded as `CROWD_MP3` by `scripts/embed-crowd.py`) play under them, never instead of them, through `crowdPlay(kind)`: a cheer for a right answer (a big cheer for several slots or slot 10), a groan for a wrong answer or time up, boos with every yellow (softer) and red card, a roar for a full board, applause at full time (a groan for a solo game on 0). Each kind's volume (`CROWD_VOL`) is set from its measured level so the crowd sits under the chimes; a new crowd sound fades out the last, and mute stops it. Whistles: one short blast for every card and for time up; the three-blast whistle (`sfx.fullTime`) is only for the end of the game.
- Stats: win rate and win streaks count only games with an opponent, people or TenaBot (`multi`). Solo games count towards games, best score, average and full boards, and the stats panel says so.
- End of game: `endGame()` builds the final table, records stats (`recordGame`), then runs `trophyReveal()` which hands the winner pill into first place. The trophy sequence is a signed-off copy of `tenable-animation.html`: keep its artwork, timings, colours and effects identical to the preview. `closeTrophy()` stops everything in the overlay (animations, the ribbons' SVG ripple, the idle bolt timer, leftover confetti) whenever it closes; `tests/trophy.test.mjs` checks the sequence and the hand-over. Tapping a row in the final table opens that player's stats for this game (right, wrong, percentage correct, best round, cards, passes, time-outs, full boards, round by round; `gameStatsHTML`), counted in `p.g` by the apply functions so every phone in an online game agrees; a small link under them swaps to the player's all-time stats (`statsHTML`, the account's for a signed-in player). A solo game that ends on 0 points has no winner: no trophy and `-` instead of a place in the table and the share message (`tests/game.test.mjs`). For First touch and Beat the clock the results card is just the final table in the players' gradients with a black line crown on the winner (`modeCard`), and the message is short (no medals). Every game ends with a banter line under the table and in the share message (`banterLine`, `banterKind`, lines in `BANTER`; Craig wants it sarcastic, rude and crude). The rules work on shares of the game's slots (rounds x 10): solo goes by the player's share (0, poor, mid, good, great, perfect); multiplayer takes the first rule that fits: everyone on 0, a tie for first, a table that found hardly anything, a chasm between first and last, a runaway winner, last place on 0, a close finish, then the winner's own score. The pick is seeded from the result, so every phone in an online game shows the same line, and each phone skips lines it showed recently (`tenaball-banter`). Add lines to any list freely; placeholders are listed above `BANTER`.
- Accounts and online games: the last `<script>` in `index.html` talks to the server in `server/` (a Cloudflare Worker). `TENABALL_SERVER` switches them on (null means offline, as before). Accounts are an email plus a 4-digit PIN; there's no email checking or PIN recovery by email, Craig resets PINs from the server's `/admin` page. The sign-in email box is `autocomplete="email"` (not `username`, which on iPhone only offers saved passwords) and is filled with the last email signed in on the phone (`EMAIL_KEY`, `tenaball-email`). Stats: `recordGame` keeps local stats for everyone except the signed-in player, whose results go to their account through `accountRecord`.
- Online play is a replayed move log. The server's room puts moves in one order, checks the sender is in the game, and lets only the host send `round`, `reveal`, `refresh`, `next` and `skip`; players send `guess`, `pass` and `timeout`. Every phone applies them in order through `applyAction`, which calls the same `applyGuess`, `applyPass`, `applyTimeout`, `applyReveal`, `applyRefresh` and `applyNext` a local game uses. Keep those deterministic: no randomness, clock reads or local-only state in them, or phones will drift apart. Question picking happens only on the host, and the chosen id travels in the move.
- Tests: `cd tests && npm install && (cd ../server && npm install) && npm test` runs the stats tests, the server tests, the game and modes tests, the account and multi-phone online tests (against a local copy of the server) and the trophy tests. `ONLY=text node <suite>` runs just the checks whose names contain the text. Run them after changing the server, the online code, the game loop or the end of game.
- Sharing: `prepareShare()` runs from `endGame()`. It builds the WhatsApp-style message (`shareText`, where `*text*` is bold) and draws the results card image (`shareCard`, 1080 by 1350) ahead of time, so tapping Share opens the share sheet straight away. `og-image.jpg` plus the `og:` meta tags give the link preview; it is the one asset kept outside `index.html`, because link previews need a real image URL.
